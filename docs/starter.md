# The starter

The other way to fill a form: tell it who this is.

> Raster Forinth is a seventh-level alchemy apprentice in the fortified mountain city of Minarok. He's
> 27 years old and he was born in 661. He had a wife and two children, but they left him because she
> grew ever-more afraid of the increasingly dangerous nature of his reckless experiments. He originally
> was a star apprentice, but he's become increasingly shunned as his ideas have grown more radical. He
> is now only embraced by the outlaw vanguard of the guild.

Press **Fill** after writing that, and every field is an attempt to write *him* down: the trade from
his apprenticeship, the birth year he was given, a personal history that runs through the family who
left, a reputation that follows from being shunned.

It sits at the top of the form, on a **new article or a stub** — the two cases where nothing has been
written yet. An article you are editing already says what it is.

## It does not replace the roll. It replaces the need for one

[The rolled skeleton](rolled-skeleton.md) exists because a model asked to invent a person reaches for
the most salient one the world allows, every time: in a maritime world, a sea captain who died crossing
the ocean. A die picking the trade, the birthplace and the lifespan is what stops that.

An author who has written a starter has already made those choices. So **a starter turns the roll off
for that article**, and nothing is rolled — not the values, not the omissions.

| | From nothing | From a starter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trade, birthplace, years, names | rolled in code | taken from what you wrote |
| Standing — how close to what the world is about | rolled, 75% not at all | whatever you described |
| Fields most articles do not have | [rolled away](fill-rates.md) | all offered, and filled from your text |

Both are still there, and they answer different questions. *Give me somebody from this world* is the
roll. *Write down the person I am already thinking about* is the starter.

Turning the roll off matters more than it sounds. The two fight otherwise: you write that he is
twenty-seven and born in 661, and a die that has already chosen 432 and a cooper's trade puts them on
the form before your words reach the model.

## What it outranks, and what it does not

It outranks the roll, the fill rates, and the model's own instincts about what a person in this world
usually is.

It does not outrank the canon. A starter asking for something a world's natural laws forbid is a
contradiction, and writing it in would make the tool worse than useless — the article would be trusted.
The skill is told to write what fits the world and say what it changed.

Fields you have already filled still win over both: those are the author's words twice over.

## It is not a field

The starter is not saved, does not appear on the article, and is not returned by generation. It is
input to one act of writing, and lives as long as the form is open.

Regenerate uses it too, so a single field rewritten an hour later still knows who it is writing about —
as long as the form has not been closed since.
